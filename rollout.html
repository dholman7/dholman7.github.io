Rolling this out to a 40-team org

The portfolio proves each tool works on one app. This is the plan for 40 product teams: what turns on when, what each check has to earn before it can block a merge, who owns what, what it costs, and what happens when it goes wrong.

Unit costs and measured rates on this page are recomputed from the repos by the same CI check as the portfolio page. Org totals are those unit costs times the volumes stated next to them.

What rolls out

  • Contract tests and can-i-deploy between each team's services and their consumers, with a long-lived broker that records deployments to each environment.
  • review-loop on pull requests, posting only comments that quote real code and survive a second check.
  • Storywright on stories that have Gherkin acceptance criteria: a verified test, opened as a pull request for a person to review.
  • agent-eval-gate on every change to the agents themselves (a prompt, a model, a tool), so a regression never reaches teams.
  • ci-context-mcp for anyone using an AI assistant: read-only CI context, scoped to the repos a team allows.

False-positive budgets, and how many PRs it takes to prove one

A check that blocks a merge wrongly costs more trust than it saves, so each blocking check gets a budget, and it stays advisory until the data shows it's within budget. The hard part is sample size. With zero false alarms in n tries, the 95% upper bound on the rate is about 3/n. Showing a rate under 1% therefore takes about 300 clean reviews in a row.

Who owns what

  • A small quality-platform team owns the tools: the harness, the gate, the transports, the red teams, the broker, and the model and prompt versions. Agent changes ship through agent-eval-gate like any other code.
  • Product teams own their scenarios. Their Gherkin is the specification Storywright writes against and the benchmark's tasks come from. Seeded bugs and references are reviewed with the team that owns the code.
  • Benchmarks are versioned and frozen. A new version each quarter takes held-out tasks from teams' new scenarios, written before any agent sees them, by people who don't tune the agent. That's how v3 was made.
  • Each consumer team owns its contracts, and each provider team owns verification and its provider states. The platform team owns the broker and the deploy gate.

Model spend is not the cost that matters. Even all-Opus is about $2,600 a year for 40 teams, less than a day of one engineer's time a quarter. The real costs are review time on the PRs Storywright opens and the trust lost to a wrong blocking comment. That's why the budgets above, not the bill, decide what blocks. Per-task caps ($1 a story) and a monthly org alert keep a runaway loop from becoming a surprise.

review-loop's trust ladder

  1. Shadow. Findings are logged, not posted, and the team labels them. It moves up when precision is measured with an interval narrow enough to decide on.
  2. Advisory comments. Posted, never required, with a "wrong" button. It moves up per category, not all at once.
  3. Blocking for one category. Starts with the highest-precision categories, such as tenant isolation and auth checks. It's required only on paths the team marks high-risk.
  4. Required check on high-risk paths. Demoted automatically to advisory if a week's dismissed-as-wrong rate exceeds the budget.

Each step down is automatic, and each step up is a decision someone signs. The red team stays part of the ladder: the reviewer's defense against a PR description that argues a breaking change is fine is measured, and every new attack found in the wild becomes a case.

What to watch

  • Per check: precision (1 − the dismissed-as-wrong rate) with its interval, and the coverage of blocking categories.
  • Per team: time to merge before and after, the share of Storywright PRs merged with no edits, and escaped defects in areas with generated tests.
  • Per agent release: the gate's result on blind held-out tasks, cost per trial, and the red team's attack rate.
  • Org-wide: model spend against the alert, and how often can-i-deploy blocks, by cause.
